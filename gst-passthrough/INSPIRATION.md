# Part F technique pass

Done 8 September 2026, after the analysis and before the writing. That is later than
the brief asks for. The reason is that the finding changed twice during the analysis,
and a technique pass against the wrong finding would have been wasted. Recorded as a
departure.

Stealing technique, not subject matter or phrasing.

## Source 1. Quanta Magazine, "Gravitational Waves Discovered at Long Last", 11 February 2016

https://www.quantamagazine.org/gravitational-waves-discovered-at-long-last-20160211/
Outside economics and finance, as the brief requires.

What the first sentence does: announces the result and its age in one breath, so the
reader knows both what happened and why it took a century.

Where the first number arrives: not in the first paragraph. The opening establishes
stakes; the number is a team size, which is scale rather than physics.

What made the smallness concrete: the piece does not say the signal was small. It
says the waves "stretch and squeeze each mile of space by a minuscule fraction of the
width of an atomic nucleus", and gives measurement precision as a thousandth of the
width of a proton. An abstraction with a body attached, exactly as Part D asks.

Where the caveat sits: mid-narrative, not at the top. The piece establishes that
everyone was sceptical, and names two past false alarms, AFTER the reader is invested
and BEFORE the verification section. The scepticism earns the verification rather
than apologising for it.

## Source 2. LIGO Scientific Collaboration, "A guide to LIGO-Virgo detector noise and extraction of transient gravitational-wave signals"

https://ligo.org/science-summaries/dataanalysisguide/
Also outside economics. This is the closest structural match to my problem that I
found anywhere: a signal genuinely smaller than the noise around it.

The move worth stealing: the residual reveal. Show the raw data with the excess in
it, then show the same data with the fitted signal subtracted, and nothing is left.
The absence in the second picture is the proof. It is far more convincing than any
statistic, because the reader sees the thing vanish.

Second move: the network argument. The signal is correlated across detectors, the
noise is not. That converts an abstract detection problem into a concrete question
about where to look.

## Source 3. Ministry of Trade and Industry, Consumer Price Developments, monthly

https://www.mti.gov.sg/newsroom
A public statistics agency, per the brief's list of places worth searching, and the
one whose voice this piece is arguing with.

The habit worth stealing: MTI names its confound in the same sentence as its result.
"the one-off effect of the 1%-point GST increase as well as seasonal effects
associated with the Chinese New Year". No hedge, no footnote, no separate limitations
paragraph. The caveat is grammatically inside the claim. That is cheaper for the
reader than a caveat parked three paragraphs later, and it is more honest, because it
cannot be skipped.

## What I am taking

STRUCTURAL, from source 2. The residual reveal. The piece shows the reader the
aggregate index first, where the tax cannot be found, and only then narrows to the
handful of prices where it can. The order is not decorative: the failure to find it
in the aggregate is what makes the water result worth anything. Show the absence,
then show the presence.

VISUAL, from source 1. Make the smallness physical before making it statistical. The
tax on a four-dollar-fifty plate of chicken rice is four cents. Ordinary January
movement in that same plate is larger. Give the reader that before any chart of log
points.

SENTENCE-LEVEL, from source 3. Name the confound inside the sentence that carries the
claim, not in a later paragraph. Applied throughout, and hardest at the point where I
have to say the water result is exploratory rather than pre-registered.

## What I did not take

The default six-function structure survives. Nothing I found beat it for this piece,
and the residual reveal fits inside it rather than replacing it: it just fixes the
order of "what the data says" so that the null comes before the positive result.
